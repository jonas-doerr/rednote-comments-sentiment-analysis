
# Codebook: RedNote Comment Analysis

## Overview

This codebook defines the thematic categories used to analyze comments on six RedNote videos. The categories were developed to examine differences in audience interaction between two types of content:

- **Chinese Media:** Videos discussing Chinese literature, culture, and society.
- **Chinglish Challenge:** Videos involving Chinglish, language challenges, and internet humor.

Each comment was manually coded using the categories below.

## Coding Procedure

- Each row represents one comment.
- Coding uses a binary system: `1` indicates that a theme is present; `0` indicates that it is absent.
- Multiple categories may be assigned to a single comment.
- Categories are not mutually exclusive.
- Comments that do not clearly fit another category may be classified as `OTHER`.

## Thematic Categories

| Code | Category | Operational Definition |
|---|---|---|
| HUM | Humor and Humorous References | Jokes, memes, playful expressions, humorous references, and attempts to participate in a humorous exchange. |
| CULT | Cultural, Historical, and Social Discussion | Discussion of Chinese culture, history, literature, society, institutions, or broader social issues. |
| LANG | Language Discussion | Comments primarily concerning language, including vocabulary, pronunciation, grammar, translation, and language learning. |
| ID | Identity and Intercultural Relations | Expressions concerning cultural identity, nationality, cross-cultural comparisons, stereotypes, and relationships between cultural groups. |
| EXP | Personal Experience and Reaction | Personal anecdotes, experiences, opinions, or substantive individual reactions to the video or its subject. |
| APP | Appreciation and Encouragement | Compliments, praise, expressions of support, gratitude, and encouragement directed toward the creator or others. |
| QST | Questions | Direct or implied requests for information, clarification, or explanation. |
| ADV | Advice and Recommendations | Suggestions, instructions, recommendations, or guidance offered to the creator or other commenters. |
| OTHER | Other or Unclear | Substantive comments that do not clearly fit any of the defined categories. |
| REACT | Minimal Reaction | Brief, low-content reactions such as generic laughter, emojis, or short expressions that do not communicate a more developed idea. |

## Coding Guidelines

### HUM vs. REACT
Use `HUM` when a comment participates in or contributes to a joke, meme, or humorous exchange. Use `REACT` for brief reactions, such as generic laughter or emojis, without additional substantive content. Both codes may apply when appropriate.

### CULT vs. ID
Use `CULT` for discussion of cultural, historical, or social topics. Use `ID` when a comment concerns identity, nationality, intercultural relationships, or comparisons between cultural groups. Both may apply when a comment addresses both subject matter and cultural identity.

### LANG vs. ADV
Use `LANG` when the comment discusses language itself. Use `ADV` when it offers advice or recommendations. A comment explaining how to pronounce a word, for example, may receive both codes.

### EXP vs. APP
Use `EXP` for personal experiences and substantive individual reactions. Use `APP` for praise, encouragement, or expressions of support. A comment may receive both codes if it shares a personal experience while also praising the creator.

## Methodological Notes

This codebook was developed for an exploratory analysis of comments from six RedNote videos. The categories reflect recurring patterns observed in the dataset rather than an established universal classification system.

Because the coding process is manual and categories may overlap, the results should be interpreted as descriptive patterns rather than objective or exhaustive classifications of audience behavior.

The dataset contains a relatively small number of comments and videos. Consequently, findings should not be generalized to all RedNote users or content categories.
